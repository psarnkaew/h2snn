FINAL METHOD (2026-07-22): SheafCovPool

What it is. A drop-in replacement for the terminal graph-classification readout (alongside GramPool/SheafPool in graph_pooling.py), not a hierarchy. It pools the backbone's existing per-node and per-edge tensors directly — no re-running the backbone at a coarser resolution, no node selection, no coarse sheaf.

Why it exists

  • GramPool (existing baseline) stacks three per-node views ($x^\star, h^0,$ pooled edges $\to X\in\mathbb R^{d\times 3}$), forms the exactly $O(d)$-invariant Gram $X^\top X\in\mathbb R^{3\times 3}$, and mean-pools it over nodes. It is cheap and correct but discards the distribution of node states — only the average survives.
  • SheafPool (HetSheaf, arXiv:2409.08036) adds whitening + Procrustes alignment + attention, but whitening ($Z=S^{-1/2}X$) needs enough stalk channels to whiten across. On H2SNN's 3-view/single-channel stacks it collapses to a near-constant descriptor — confirmed empirically twice (Phase-0 sweep and the final gate below): ~0.26 macro-F1 on MUTAG, at/below the majority-class floor.
  • SheafCovPool keeps GramPool's exactly-invariant, whitening-free Gram descriptor, and adds the two ingredients GramPool lacks without introducing SheafPool's failure mode: a learned gauge-invariant attention weighting, and the across-node (and across-edge) covariance of the (attention-projected) descriptor — not just its mean — so the readout can represent how spread out node/edge states are, not only their average.

Mechanism

For each node $v$, stack the same three views GramPool uses into $H_v\in\mathbb R^{d\times 3}$ (edges use $[\delta_0 x^\star, \delta_0 h^0, s^\star]$ analogously). Compute the flattened Gram descriptor

The node stack's third view is $\widetilde s_v^\star=\frac{1}{d_v}\sum_{e\ni v}F_{v\unlhd e}^{\top}s_e^\star$, not the plain mean of incident $s_e^\star$ that the original implementation used. Each incident edge state lives in its own edge stalk, so averaging the raw coordinates and then taking inner products against $x_v^\star,h_v^{(0)}$ is not a well-defined sheaf operation, and it broke the invariance claimed below for the three Gram entries touching that column. Pulling each state back through its own endpoint restriction map first gives $(R_eFQ_v^{\top})^{\top}R_es=Q_vF^{\top}s$, so all three columns share one gauge. The edge stack was already correct — its three views live in a single stalk. See sheafcovpool_implementation.md §2a.

$$g_v = \mathrm{vec}(H_v^\top H_v)\in\mathbb R^{9},\qquad u_v = \mathrm{MLP}(g_v)\in\mathbb R^{F}\ (F{=}8),$$

score it with a learned gauge-invariant attention head and softmax over the nodes of each graph in the batch, $\alpha_v=\mathrm{softmax}_{v\in\text{graph}}(\mathrm{MLP}(u_v))$, then pool both moments:

$$\mu_g=\sum_{v}\alpha_v u_v,\qquad \Sigma_g=\sum_v \alpha_v\,(u_v-\mu_g)(u_v-\mu_g)^\top,$$

keep the upper triangle of $\Sigma_g$ (incl. diagonal; $F(F{+}1)/2{=}36$ dims), do the same for the edge descriptor, concatenate $[\mu^{\text{node}}_g,\ \mathrm{triu}(\Sigma^{\text{node}}_g),\ \mu^{\text{edge}}_g,\ \mathrm{triu}(\Sigma^{\text{edge}}_g)]$, and classify with an MLP head. Trained with cross-entropy only, same discipline as GramPool. Both moments are exactly invariant to any per-node/per-edge $O(d)$ gauge change, since they are built entirely from the already-invariant Gram descriptor $g_v$ — no whitening, no alignment, no pseudo-inverse.

Decision summary — what we will build (superseded — see card above)

  • One method, one loss. A Select–Reduce–Connect pooling layer trained with cross-entropy only — matching the loss discipline of HINPool, MVPool, and HiSP.
  • Pool all three ranks. Node ($C^0$), edge ($C^1$), and face ($C^2$) cochains. Faces are first-class, not deferred — justified by the measurement in §3.
  • Select = type-aware Top-$K$ per node type, scored by gauge-invariant sheaf quantities (node energy, edge disagreement, face curl). No Gumbel/slot menu.
  • Reduce/Connect = induced typed sub-complex, re-fed to the H2SNN backbone, which re-derives coarse restriction maps. No transport, no polar projection, no constructed coarse coboundaries.
  • Gauge = $O(d)$ descriptors; conformal map-scale carried explicitly (the deployed maps are scaled-orthogonal, not orthogonal — §5).
  • Scope = TU graph classification only (MUTAG, PROTEINS, ENZYMES, NCI1). No HIN, synthetic, node, or link tasks in this paper.

Cut from earlier drafts as unnecessary or premature: gauge transport + polar projection, constructed $\delta'_0/\delta'_1$ with Galerkin/commuting/truncation/realization losses, the seven-term loss, and the neutral/adaptive/stress-test heterophily modes. See §11 for the explicit list and why each was removed.

1. Why replace the legacy S2Pool

The current terminal pooling in src/models/graph_pooling.py stacks three local views into $X\in\mathbb R^{d\times 3}$ and computes the projector

$$P(X)=X^\top(XX^\top)^\dagger X.$$

When $d\ge 3$ and $X$ has full column rank, the row space of $X$ is all of $\mathbb R^3$, so $P(X)=I_3$ identically — a constant, independent of the data. For generic local states the descriptor therefore carries no graph-class information, and the operator produces only graph logits: it builds no smaller graph and no coarse sheaf. It cannot be the main contribution.

The replacement must be a genuine hierarchy that maps a typed H2 sheaf complex at resolution $\ell$

$$(\mathcal X^{(\ell)},x^{0,(\ell)},x^{1,(\ell)},x^{2,(\ell)})\ \longmapsto\ (\mathcal X^{(\ell+1)},x^{0,(\ell+1)},x^{1,(\ell+1)},x^{2,(\ell+1)})$$

so that another H2SNN sheaf-Hodge block can run at the coarser resolution, and the final graph representation is permutation- and gauge-invariant.

Naming. Legacy S2Pool = the terminal pseudoinverse projector in graph_pooling.py (kept only as a diagnostic baseline). THS-Pool = the committed hierarchical method defined here.

2. Positioning and novelty

The three closest methods are each one committed pipeline trained with a single cross-entropy loss. THS-Pool follows that discipline and differs in exactly one structural way: it pools the ranks the others cannot.

The decisive row is edge state to pool. HiSP and NSD diffuse only node features; the edge stalk exists only implicitly inside the Laplacian, so there is no 1-cochain signal to pool and no face rank at all. H2SNN is different: it maintains an explicit edge cochain $s^\star$ (via EdgeInitializer + HodgeDiffusion) and an explicit face coboundary $\delta_1 s^\star$. Pooling the edge and face cochains is therefore something the prior sheaf-pooling work structurally cannot do — that is the novelty, and §3 shows those ranks carry real signal on the target datasets.

Do not claim “first hierarchical sheaf pooling.” HiSP owns that. Our defensible claim is typed, higher-order ($0\!\to\!1\!\to\!2$) cochain coarsening on directed HINs, trained end to end with a single loss. Even HiSP keeps 2-cells out of its trained method (its “loop-cell” appears only in the exact-realization analysis) — so pooling faces as a first-class rank is genuinely new.

3. Motivating measurement: faces carry signal

Before committing to pool faces, we measured whether H2SNN's face construction does anything on TU graph classification. Define the fraction of edge-Hodge ($L_1$) energy coming from the face/upper term:

$$ \kappa=\frac{\langle s^\star,\ \delta_1^\ast\delta_1\, s^\star\rangle}{\langle s^\star,\ L_1\, s^\star\rangle} =\frac{\|\delta_1 s^\star\|^2}{\|\delta_1 s^\star\|^2+\|\delta_0^\ast s^\star\|^2}, \qquad L_1=\delta_0\delta_0^\ast+\delta_1^\ast\delta_1 . $$

$\kappa\approx 0$ would mean the face rank is inert and could be dropped. We trained the backbone with grampool (which reads $s^\star$; note $x$ and $s$ diffuse independently in diffusion.py, so meanpool is blind to faces), discovery off (clean typed 2-hop 2-cells), single holdout split, and measured $\kappa$ at initialization and after training.

DatasetNode types$\kappa$ init → trainedInterpretation
MUTAG7 atom (aromatic rings)0.17 → 0.41 ↑training amplifies the face term
PROTEINS3 SSE0.23 → 0.042 ↓training suppresses the face term
Conclusion. On molecular, ring-bearing graphs the model routes ~40% of edge-Hodge energy through faces and increases that during training — the face rank is used, not inert. On coarse 3-type protein graphs it is suppressed. So faces are needed conditionally, and the model itself decides. This is the empirical motivation for pooling the face rank, and $\kappa$ is the measurable condition that says when it should matter.

Honesty bounds: this is a single split; the faces-on vs faces-off accuracy delta was noise-level at TU scale, so the claim rests on the energy diagnostic $\kappa$, not on an accuracy win. NCI1 (molecular, ~37 atom types) was expected to track MUTAG but its run was not completed. Repro: scratchpad/measure_faces.py.

4. Typed H2 problem definition

Each graph is a typed oriented complex with node types $\tau$, relation types $\rho$, and face schemas $\phi$:

$$C^0=\bigoplus_{v}\mathcal F(v),\quad C^1=\bigoplus_{e}\mathcal F(e),\quad C^2=\bigoplus_{f}\mathcal F(f), \qquad \delta_0:C^0\!\to\!C^1,\quad \delta_1:C^1\!\to\!C^2.$$

H2SNN's backbone already exposes everything the pooler consumes:

QuantityMeaningRank
$x^\star$final diffused node cochain0
$s^\star$final diffused edge cochain1
$\delta_0 x^\star$node→edge disagreement1
$\delta_1 s^\star$edge→face curl2
$R_{v\to e}=\lambda_{v\to e}O_{v\to e}$conformal restriction maps0→1

The task is graph classification: one label per graph, predicted from a permutation- and gauge-invariant multiresolution readout.

5. Gauge contract

The gauge group is $O(d)$: an orthonormal change of stalk basis $x_c\mapsto G_c x_c$, $G_c\in O(d)$. Under it, Gram descriptors $H^\top H$ and energy norms are exactly invariant. The pooler's selection scores are built only from such invariants; raw stalk coordinates never enter a score.

Conformal-scale correction (committed handling). The deployed restriction maps are scaled orthogonal, $R=\lambda O$ with a learned per-edge $\lambda=\exp(\cdot)>0$ (sheaf_maps.py, confirmed by the backbone documentation's own limitation note) — not orthogonal. Consequences we commit to:
  • Node/edge/face energy descriptors are $O(d)$-invariant because the conformal scale lives in the cochain values, not the basis. These are safe to use as selection features.
  • We do not build node-to-node transport $R_{v\to e}^\top R_{u\to e}$ (which would be a scaled rotation whose scale a polar projection silently deletes). The induced sub-complex in §6 avoids transport entirely, so this failure mode cannot occur.
  • The per-edge scale $\lambda$ is exposed to the readout as an explicit invariant scalar feature, so its information is retained rather than discarded.
General $GL(d)$ gauge is out of scope.

6. The committed method

Method update (2026-07-21): THS-Pool is now energy-preserving Galerkin coarsening. The Select–Reduce–Connect / re-diffuse design described in the rest of this section was implemented and tested; it either did not coarsen the sheaf (re-learning the maps) or coarsened it inconsistently (injecting maps, which hurt). The method now uses the Galerkin construction the proposal always specified (§10.3): a prolongation $P$ and coarse operator $$L_c = P^{*} L P,\qquad z^{*}L_c z = \lVert\delta (Pz)\rVert^{2},$$ so the coarse energy equals the fine sheaf energy of the lifted signal — energy-consistent by construction, and lighter (it re-uses the fine $L$; no per-level backbone re-run). Build node-Galerkin first, then the higher-order $L_1$ term (the novelty vs HiSP). Authoritative spec: sheafcovpool_implementation.md; plain-language walkthrough: sheafcovpool_explainer.md. The SRC text below is retained as history.

(Historical — the original Select–Reduce–Connect design.) One Select–Reduce–Connect layer, inserted between H2SNN blocks. It is the sheaf-native analogue of HINPool's type-aware pooling: same skeleton, but the selection score and the readout are driven by gauge-invariant sheaf quantities the ordinary methods cannot compute, and it coarsens all three cochain ranks.

One clarification that governs the whole method: THS-Pool selects nodes; the edge and face cochains are recomputed by the backbone at each resolution and read out — they are not transported across levels. "Pool three ranks" means "read out three ranks per resolution," not "carry three cochains downward."

Invariant node/edge/face energy views.

6.1 Invariant cell descriptors

For node $v$ of type $t=\tau(v)$, the selection feature vector concatenates only $O(d)$-invariants:

$$ z_v=\Big[\ \|x_v^\star\|^2\ \big\Vert\ \operatorname{mean}_{e\ni v}\epsilon_e\ \big\Vert\ \operatorname{mean}_{f\ni v}\|(\delta_1 s^\star)_f\|^2\ \big\Vert\ \operatorname{mean}_{e\ni v}\log\lambda_e\ \big\Vert\ \sigma\!\big(a\log(\deg v+\varepsilon)+b\big)\ \Big], $$

with the gauge-invariant edge residual

$$\epsilon_e=\big\|R_{u\to e}x_u^\star-R_{v\to e}x_v^\star\big\|^2,\qquad e=(u,v).$$

These are the sheaf analogues of MVPool's three views (feature, structure, structure+feature), but every term is gauge-invariant, and the third term is the face-curl energy whose relevance §3 established. The score is a type-specific MLP, $\;p_v=\sigma\big(\operatorname{MLP}_{\tau(v)}(z_v)\big)$.

6.2 Select — type-aware Top-$K$

For graph $g$ and node type $t$ with $n_{g,t}$ nodes, keep

$$K_{g,t}=\begin{cases}0,& n_{g,t}=0,\\[2pt]\min\!\big(K_t^{\max},\ \max(1,\lceil \rho_t\, n_{g,t}\rceil)\big),& n_{g,t}>0,\end{cases}$$

the $K_{g,t}$ highest-scored nodes of each type (deterministic Top-$K$, as in HINPool's Type-Aware Selector). Absent types create no supernodes. Retained node states are score-gated to keep selection differentiable, $\;\hat x_v^\star = p_v\, x_v^\star$ (HINPool's gating trick). Training and evaluation both use Top-$K$; there is no Gumbel/prototype alternative in the method.

6.3 Reduce / Connect — induced typed sub-complex

The coarse nodes and edges are the sub-complex induced on the kept nodes: kept nodes are the coarse nodes; an edge survives iff both endpoints survive (relation type and direction preserved). Faces are not induced from fine faces — they are re-enumerated on the coarse topology by the same build_typed_path_face_groups builder used at level 0, so their edge indices are valid by construction. This is the sheaf version of the SAGPool/MVPool induced-subgraph connect step. No cluster-assignment matrix, no transport, no constructed coarse coboundary.

Backbone contract (committed). The coarse complex is re-encoded through encode_from_stalk() — a thin coarse-entry variant of encode() that skips the input node-encoder, because coarse nodes carry $d$-dimensional stalks rather than raw in_channels features (this is a required, behavior-preserving backbone refactor; see the implementation spec Appendix A). It re-derives the coarse restriction maps from the pooled stalks and consumes the re-enumerated coarse faces, so higher-order diffusion ($\delta_1/L_1$) is preserved at every resolution without the pooler building $\delta'_0/\delta'_1$ itself. Injecting constructed coarse coboundaries into the backbone is explicit future work, not part of this paper.

6.4 Multiresolution invariant readout

At each level $\ell$, form one invariant summary per rank, aggregated by type / relation / schema:

$$ g_0^{(\ell)}=\bigoplus_{t}\ \textstyle\sum_{\tau(v)=t}\varphi_t\big(\|x_v^\star\|^2\big),\quad g_1^{(\ell)}=\bigoplus_{r}\ \textstyle\sum_{\rho(e)=r}\psi_r\big(\epsilon_e,\ \log\lambda_e\big),\quad g_2^{(\ell)}=\bigoplus_{q}\ \textstyle\sum_{\phi(f)=q}\xi_q\big(\|(\delta_1 s^\star)_f\|^2\big). $$

The level token is $g^{(\ell)}=[\,g_0^{(\ell)}\Vert g_1^{(\ell)}\Vert g_2^{(\ell)}\,]$, and levels are fused by concatenation:

$$h_G=\big[\,g^{(0)}\Vert g^{(1)}\Vert\cdots\Vert g^{(L)}\,\big],\qquad \hat y_G=\operatorname{MLP}_{\mathrm{cls}}(h_G).$$

The face summary $g_2$ is where the rank whose relevance §3 established enters the graph representation — the component HiSP/HINPool/MVPool cannot form. Concatenation is the committed fusion; attention fusion is an ablation, not a claim.

7. Training objective

$$\mathcal L=\operatorname{CrossEntropy}(\hat y_G,\ y_G).$$

That is the entire loss — matching HINPool, MVPool, and HiSP (whose Galerkin/truncation/realization quantities are analysis, never training terms). We deliberately do not add smoothness, chain-map, boundary, Hodge-energy, or mass-preservation losses: the coarse sheaf is re-derived by the backbone (§6.3), so there is no constructed operator to regularize, and a single-loss method is what makes the contribution legible rather than a tuning surface. If Top-$K$ selection proves unstable in practice, at most a single standard pooling regularizer (link/entropy) may be added and reported as an ablation — not baked into the headline method.

Rejected auxiliary losses. The spectral term $\|U_k^\top U_k-\widetilde U_k^\top\widetilde U_k\|_F^2$ is identically zero for orthonormal bases and must never be used. The commuting-diagram and Galerkin energy losses from earlier drafts are removed with the constructed coarse operators they regularized.

8. Algorithm and interface

8.1 Forward

  1. Run an H2SNN block → $x^\star,s^\star$, restriction maps $R$, face caches, $\delta_0 x^\star,\delta_1 s^\star$.
  2. Build invariant node/edge/face descriptors (§6.1) and the level-$\ell$ readout token (§6.4).
  3. Score nodes and select Top-$K$ per node type; score-gate kept states (§6.2).
  4. Induce the coarse nodes and typed edges; re-enumerate coarse faces on that topology (§6.3).
  5. Re-encode the coarse complex via encode_from_stalk() (maps re-derived; §6.3 card).
  6. Repeat for $L$ levels; concatenate level readout tokens; classify.

8.2 PyTorch interface

class S2PoolH2Level(NamedTuple):

    node_type: Tensor     # [N_coarse]
    edge_index: Tensor    # [2, E_coarse]
    edge_type: Tensor     # [E_coarse]
    batch: Tensor         # [N_coarse]      graph id per coarse node
    level_readout: Tensor # [num_graphs, readout_dim]
    perm: Tensor          # [N_coarse]      kept fine-node ids (analysis / equivariance tests)

class S2PoolH2(nn.Module):
    def forward(self, encoded: EncodedState,
                face_groups: tuple[FaceGroup, ...],
                edge_index: Tensor, edge_type: Tensor,
                node_type: Tensor, batch: Tensor) -> S2PoolH2Level: ...

The output carries the coarse graph (nodes, typed edges) plus gated node stalks and the level readout token — not a pooled edge cochain. Edge and face cochains are recomputed by the next encode_from_stalk(); coarse faces are re-enumerated by the driver per level (mirroring level 0). 9.1 Datasets

MUTAG, PROTEINS, ENZYMES, NCI1 (src/data/graph_classification_dataset.py) — the repository's current graph-classification benchmarks. No other datasets in this paper.

  • H2SNN + mean pooling; H2SNN + GramPool; H2SNN + legacy S2Pool projector;
  • HINPool and MVPool (typed / multi-view Top-$K$ poolers);
  • HiSP (closest hierarchical sheaf baseline, node-only);
  • a standard hierarchical pooler (SAGPool / DiffPool);
  • THS-Pool rank ablations: node-only, node+edge, node+edge+face.

9.4 Statistical protocol

TU sample sizes are small ($n\approx 188$–$4110$) and 10-fold CV variance is high, so:
  • Pre-register one primary comparison per RQ before running; everything else is exploratory.
  • Fix the seed budget (e.g. 10 folds × 5 seeds) and report mean ± std and a paired effect size with a bootstrap CI, not just point accuracy.
  • Match parameters and compute between THS-Pool and the terminal-readout control so a win is not just added capacity.
  • Correct for multiplicity (Holm/BH) across exploratory comparisons.

9.5 Metrics

  • Accuracy, ROC-AUC, macro-F1 as appropriate; mean ± std over seeds.
  • $\kappa$ per dataset (the higher-order-utility diagnostic).
  • Permutation / gauge / conformal-scale invariance error.
  • Params, runtime, peak memory, retained-cell ratio.

12. Correctness tests

  1. Legacy degeneracy regression: the old projector becomes $I_3$ for random full-rank stacks.
  2. Permutation equivariance: reindex fine cells → reindexed coarse structure/states.
  3. $O(d)$ gauge invariance: independent orthogonal gauges leave scores and graph logits unchanged.
  4. Conformal-scale handling: random per-edge positive scales leave scores unchanged and move the scale feature as expected.
  5. Type purity: no node assignment crosses the configured type mask.
  6. Relation/face preservation: coarse edges keep relation types; coarse faces keep schema and boundary order.
  7. Empty type: $n_{g,t}=0$ yields no phantom supernode, no NaN.
  8. Batch isolation: no coarse cell crosses graph boundaries in a PyG batch.
  9. Rank ladder wiring: disabling the edge or face rank removes exactly its parameters/gradients.
  10. Cap compliance: retained coarse cells respect $\rho_t,K_t^{\max}$.

13. Claims and claims to avoid

13.1 Defensible after validation

  • THS-Pool performs typed hierarchical coarsening of node, edge, and face cochains for graph classification, trained with a single loss.
  • It pools the edge and face ranks that HiSP/HINPool/MVPool structurally cannot, and §3 shows those ranks carry signal on molecular data.
  • Its scores use $O(d)$-invariant descriptors; the conformal map-scale is carried explicitly, not assumed absent.
  • It retains relation types, face schemas, and orientation at coarse resolutions.

13.2 To avoid

  • “First hierarchical sheaf pooling” (HiSP owns it).
  • “Orthogonal restriction maps” — they are conformal (scaled-orthogonal).
  • “The coarse complex is an exact algebraic coarsening” — under rederive the coarse sheaf is learned.
  • “Faces always help” — §3 shows it is conditional ($\kappa$ high on MUTAG, low on PROTEINS).
  • “The hierarchy is what wins” unless V1 beats the matched V0 terminal readout at equal params/compute.
  • “Guaranteed information preservation” under dimensionality reduction; “more expressive than SheafPool” without a theorem.
Bottom line. One committed pipeline, one loss, TU only. The single structural novelty — pooling the typed edge and face cochains of the H2 complex — is exactly the part prior sheaf/HIN poolers cannot do, and the §3 measurement is the evidence that it is worth doing.
